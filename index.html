A small team pursuing frontier research

Research Group

We are a small, student-driven research team of four researchers working under the guidance of a faculty advisor. We focus on frontier questions in large language models, aiming to develop original methods and empirical understanding rather than simply building applications.

Research Directions

  • 1. LLM Reasoning & Test-Time Scaling

    How can language models reason more effectively with additional inference-time computation? We investigate diverse reasoning trajectories, self-consistency and answer distributions, search-based inference, and the limits of test-time scaling.

  • 2. Behavioral Modeling & Social Simulation

    Can language models learn transferable behavioral patterns from limited human observations? We study learning from social experiments, generalization across people and interventions, and the reliability of behavioral and treatment-effect predictions.

Research Philosophy

We value important questions, independent thinking, and rigorous empirical evidence. Our work aims to connect scientific understanding with methodological advances, and we favor sustained progress on a few focused projects over a large collection of loosely connected ideas.

Join & Collaborate

We welcome conversations with researchers from different backgrounds and inquiries from students interested in research internships. We are especially excited by collaborations that bring together fresh questions, complementary expertise, and careful experimentation.

Research Collaboration

We are interested in collaborations on LLM reasoning, inference-time computation, and data-grounded behavioral modeling. Interdisciplinary work connecting machine learning with social science, behavioral research, and experimental methodology is particularly welcome.

If you see a concrete connection to our research, feel free to write with a brief introduction and the question you would like to explore together.

Research Internships

We welcome motivated students who want to work on frontier research problems in LLMs. Depending on project needs and mutual fit, prospective interns may contribute to problem formulation, paper reading, model experiments, and evaluation.

What we value

  • Curiosity about scientific questions and willingness to think independently.
  • A solid foundation in machine learning, with practical Python / PyTorch experience or equivalent research skills.
  • Careful experimentation, clear communication, and commitment to reproducible results.

How to reach out

Send a short email introducing yourself, your research interests, and relevant experience. A CV and links to previous projects or code are helpful. Please use the subject “Research Internship” or “Research Collaboration” so your message is easy to identify.
